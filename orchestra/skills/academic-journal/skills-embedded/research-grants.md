# Research Grant Writing (Pipeline Extract)

Grant writing is the process of developing competitive funding proposals for federal agencies and foundations. **Grants are persuasive documents** that must simultaneously demonstrate scientific rigor, innovation, feasibility, and broader impact.

## When to Use

- Writing proposals for NSF, NIH, DOE, DARPA, or NSTC programs
- Preparing project descriptions, specific aims, or technical narratives
- Developing broader impacts or significance statements
- Creating research timelines and milestone plans
- Responding to reviewer comments in resubmissions

## Agency Quick Reference

| Agency | Key Document | Page Limit | Core Criteria |
|--------|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| NSF | Project Description | 15 pages | Intellectual Merit + Broader Impacts (equal weight) |
| NIH | Research Strategy | 12 pages (R01) | Significance, Innovation, Approach, Investigator, Environment |
| DOE | Project Narrative | varies | Scientific merit, approach, team, budget, mission relevance |
| DARPA | Technical Volume | varies | High-risk high-reward, "what if you're right, who cares" |
| NSTC | CM03 Form | varies | Innovation, Feasibility, PI Capability, Value |

## Core Proposal Components

1. **Abstract/Summary** — standalone overview accessible to non-specialists
2. **Specific Aims** (NIH) / **Objectives** (NSF/DOE) — 2-4 clear, testable goals
3. **Project Description** — background, methods, timeline, preliminary data
4. **Broader Impacts** (NSF) / **Significance** (NIH) — societal/educational value
5. **Budget Justification** — personnel, equipment, travel, materials aligned with plan
6. **Team Qualifications** — biosketches, track record, facilities

## Writing Principles

- **Write for multiple audiences**: technical reviewers + program officers + panelists
- **Use active voice** and strong verbs (investigate, elucidate, discover)
- **Balance innovation with feasibility** — acknowledge risk, provide alternatives
- **Align all parts**: budget supports activities, timeline matches aims, team has expertise

## Common Mistakes

- Exceeding page limits (automatic rejection)
- Mismatch with agency mission
- Vague objectives (not specific or measurable)
- Insufficient preliminary data for NIH R01
- Poor broader impacts for NSF
